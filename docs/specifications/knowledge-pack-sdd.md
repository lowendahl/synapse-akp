# Knowledge Pack — Software Design Description

## 1. Purpose

This document defines:

1. what a **Knowledge Pack** is;
2. the principles by which source knowledge is ingested and compiled into a Knowledge Pack;
3. the architectural decisions already made for the Knowledge Pack;
4. the architectural decisions that remain open.

This document intentionally does not define how a Knowledge Pack is distributed, synchronized, served, queried, retrieved, planned over, or executed against.

## 2. Scope

The scope is deliberately narrow.

The specification covers:

- the conceptual definition of a Knowledge Pack;
- the relationship between canonical knowledge and compiled knowledge;
- the logical contents of a Knowledge Pack;
- the ingestion and compilation lifecycle;
- deterministic and probabilistic enrichment;
- provenance, reproducibility, immutability, and validation;
- accepted Architecture Decision Records (ADRs);
- open ADRs requiring future decisions.

The specification does not cover:

- planner architecture;
- execution architecture;
- runtime adapters;
- end-user applications;
- retrieval APIs;
- hosting or remote serving;
- package distribution;
- authentication or authorization;
- customer-data access;
- Power BI, Dataverse, OData, or other execution connectors;
- concrete database or vector-engine selection, except where recorded as an open decision.

## 3. Definition

A **Knowledge Pack** is an immutable, versioned, reproducible software artifact containing compiled organizational knowledge.

It is produced from one or more canonical knowledge repositories through a controlled ingestion and compilation process.

A Knowledge Pack is not the authoritative source of the knowledge it contains. It is a derived artifact optimized for machine use.

A Knowledge Pack is conceptually closer to a compiled software dependency than to an authored document repository or a live operational database.

The canonical source is authored and governed by humans. The Knowledge Pack is generated by tooling.

## 4. Core Mental Model

```text
Canonical Knowledge Repository
            │
            ▼
     Knowledge Compiler
            │
            ▼
       Knowledge Pack
```

The canonical repository expresses what the organization knows.

The compiler transforms that knowledge into validated, structured, linked, and indexed representations.

The Knowledge Pack contains the resulting intelligence assets.

The Knowledge Pack can be deleted and rebuilt. The canonical repository cannot be reconstructed reliably from the compiled pack and therefore remains the source of truth.

## 5. Goals

A Knowledge Pack shall be:

- **Compiled** — generated through a defined process rather than manually edited.
- **Immutable** — unchanged after publication.
- **Reproducible** — rebuildable from the same canonical source, compiler version, configuration, and model dependencies.
- **Versioned** — uniquely identifiable and traceable to its source state.
- **Portable** — independent of a specific planner or execution runtime.
- **Inspectable** — understandable through manifests, schemas, metadata, and provenance.
- **Validated** — checked before it is considered publishable.
- **Disposable** — safe to delete because it can be regenerated.
- **Provenance-preserving** — every compiled object can be traced to its source.
- **Implementation-neutral at the logical level** — its conceptual model is not defined by a particular database or framework.

## 6. Non-Goals

A Knowledge Pack is not:

- a general-purpose content management system;
- a collaborative authoring environment;
- a runtime memory store;
- a customer-data cache;
- a planner;
- an agent;
- an execution engine;
- a source-system authorization layer;
- a live operational database;
- a substitute for the canonical repository;
- a place where inferred knowledge is silently promoted to fact.

## 7. Engineering Principles

### KP-01 — Canonical knowledge exists outside the pack

The Knowledge Pack shall never be treated as the canonical source.

All authoritative changes shall occur in a governed source repository and flow through compilation.

### KP-02 — Knowledge Packs are compiled, not authored

Humans may author source knowledge and compiler configuration.

Humans shall not manually patch the contents of a published Knowledge Pack.

### KP-03 — Published packs are immutable

Publication creates a new version.

A published version shall never be modified in place.

### KP-04 — Every pack is reproducible

A build shall record enough information to reproduce or explain the package:

- source revision;
- compiler version;
- schema version;
- configuration version;
- ontology version;
- enrichment model versions;
- build timestamp;
- content hashes.

Probabilistic enrichment may prevent byte-for-byte reproduction unless the model, parameters, and runtime are fully deterministic. In those cases, the build shall remain semantically reproducible and explicitly identify nondeterministic stages.

### KP-05 — Derived representations remain derived

Graphs, vectors, lexical indexes, summaries, classifications, and other projections are disposable outputs.

They shall not become independent sources of truth.

### KP-06 — Deterministic processing precedes probabilistic processing

Parsing, schema validation, ID resolution, authored links, aliases, and explicit relationships shall be processed before LLM or statistical enrichment.

Probabilistic methods shall only address ambiguity or derive optional enrichment that deterministic methods cannot provide adequately.

### KP-07 — LLMs enrich but do not govern

An LLM may:

- classify;
- summarize;
- propose concepts;
- propose aliases;
- propose relationships;
- contextualize text for embeddings;
- identify possible duplicates or contradictions.

An LLM shall not:

- rewrite canonical knowledge;
- invent canonical identifiers;
- silently alter authored meaning;
- promote inferred relations to approved facts without policy;
- delete or deprecate knowledge;
- invent ontology predicates outside the allowed ontology.

### KP-08 — Provenance is mandatory

Every compiled object shall retain a trace to its origin.

At minimum, provenance shall identify:

- source repository;
- source document;
- source revision;
- source location or section;
- transformation stage;
- compiler version;
- origin type such as authored, deterministic, inferred, or generated.

### KP-09 — Stable identity is preserved

Canonical entities and sections shall use stable IDs.

Compilation shall not base identity solely on file paths, headings, array positions, generated database keys, or embedding order.

### KP-10 — One source may produce multiple projections

A single canonical knowledge object may produce:

- a structured object;
- graph nodes and edges;
- lexical entries;
- semantic chunks;
- embedding records;
- aliases;
- examples;
- procedures;
- validation records.

All projections shall point back to the same canonical identity.

### KP-11 — The pack contains organizational knowledge, not protected operational data

A general Knowledge Pack shall not contain customer-level results, user tokens, personal execution caches, row-level-security outputs, or other operational data protected by source-system authorization.

Domain examples included in a pack shall be synthetic, anonymized, or explicitly approved.

### KP-12 — Compilation fails visibly

The compiler shall not silently discard invalid knowledge.

Errors, warnings, unresolved references, and rejected enrichment shall be emitted as explicit build diagnostics.

### KP-13 — Invalid ontology usage is rejected

The compiler shall validate node types, predicates, required properties, allowed relationships, and cardinality constraints where applicable.

Unknown types or predicates shall not be silently accepted.

### KP-14 — Pack contents are implementation-neutral at the contract level

The logical definition of concepts, relations, chunks, indexes, and metadata shall not depend on a particular physical engine.

Physical technologies may vary without changing the meaning of the pack.

### KP-15 — Minimalism is preferred

The compiler shall only create an artifact when it has a clear use and traceable source.

The pack shall not accumulate speculative fields, duplicated representations, or model-generated material without an identified consumer need.

## 8. Logical Contents of a Knowledge Pack

A Knowledge Pack is defined by logical assets rather than a mandatory physical storage layout.

### 8.1 Manifest

The manifest identifies the pack and the conditions under which it was built.

It shall include, at minimum:

- pack identifier;
- pack version;
- schema version;
- source revision;
- compiler version;
- ontology version;
- build timestamp;
- asset inventory;
- content hashes;
- build status;
- compatibility metadata where defined.

### 8.2 Canonical Object Projection

The pack shall contain normalized representations of canonical knowledge objects.

Representative object types include:

- document;
- section;
- concept;
- framework;
- stage;
- principle;
- role;
- process;
- artifact;
- metric;
- outcome;
- tool definition;
- skill definition;
- procedure;
- example;
- claim;
- evidence.

The exact ontology is domain-specific and versioned independently.

### 8.3 Relationship Projection

The pack may contain relationships between objects.

Each relationship shall include:

- subject ID;
- predicate;
- object ID;
- origin;
- provenance;
- confidence where applicable;
- review status where applicable.

Representative origins are:

- authored;
- structurally derived;
- deterministically resolved;
- probabilistically inferred.

### 8.4 Graph Projection

The graph projection is a compiled view of knowledge relationships.

It may include:

- repository containment;
- document containment;
- section hierarchy;
- concept references;
- authored links;
- alias resolution;
- typed semantic relationships;
- provenance links.

The graph is a projection, not an independent authoring surface.

### 8.5 Lexical Projection

The lexical projection supports exact and term-oriented matching.

It may include:

- canonical terms;
- aliases;
- acronyms;
- normalized forms;
- headings;
- keywords;
- domain vocabulary;
- source text fragments.

### 8.6 Semantic Projection

The semantic projection contains semantically meaningful knowledge units created from the source structure.

Semantic units should align with meaning rather than arbitrary token lengths.

Representative units include:

- definitions;
- principles;
- procedures;
- constraints;
- examples;
- evidence;
- decisions.

### 8.7 Vector Projection

The vector projection contains embeddings generated from semantic units or contextualized variants of those units.

Each vector record shall reference:

- the canonical object or semantic unit;
- source provenance;
- embedding model identifier;
- embedding configuration;
- content hash;
- contextualization method where used.

The vector itself is always disposable and rebuildable.

### 8.8 Ontology Snapshot

The pack shall identify the ontology used during compilation.

The snapshot or reference shall define:

- allowed object types;
- allowed predicates;
- required fields;
- constraints;
- version.

### 8.9 Diagnostics and Build Metadata

The build output shall record:

- errors;
- warnings;
- unresolved links;
- rejected relations;
- duplicate IDs;
- alias conflicts;
- deprecated constructs;
- model-enrichment confidence;
- validation results.

Whether diagnostics are included inside the published pack or retained as an adjacent build artifact is an open decision.

## 9. Canonical Source Expectations

The initial canonical source format is expected to be structured Markdown with YAML front matter, stable identifiers, headings, authored links, and explicit metadata.

A canonical document may contain:

- stable document ID;
- document type;
- title;
- aliases;
- status;
- authority;
- owners;
- tags;
- explicit relationships;
- headings and subheadings;
- Markdown body;
- wiki-style links;
- code blocks;
- tables;
- callouts;
- examples.

The Knowledge Pack specification does not require Markdown forever. Other canonical formats may be supported if they can produce the same normalized intermediate representation and preserve equivalent provenance.

## 10. Compilation Lifecycle

```text
Canonical sources
      ↓
Discover
      ↓
Parse
      ↓
Normalize
      ↓
Validate
      ↓
Resolve identities and references
      ↓
Extract deterministic structure
      ↓
Apply optional semantic enrichment
      ↓
Create logical projections
      ↓
Validate compiled outputs
      ↓
Assemble manifest and assets
      ↓
Publish immutable Knowledge Pack
```

## 11. Compilation Stages

### 11.1 Discover

The compiler identifies the source set included in the build.

Discovery shall be governed by explicit configuration rather than accidental filesystem traversal.

The source set shall be recorded.

### 11.2 Parse

The compiler parses each source into an intermediate representation.

Parsing shall preserve:

- document boundaries;
- heading hierarchy;
- list structure;
- tables;
- links;
- metadata;
- source locations;
- relevant formatting semantics.

### 11.3 Normalize

Normalization converts source-specific syntax into common internal models.

Normalization may include:

- canonical casing;
- whitespace normalization;
- normalized link targets;
- alias normalization;
- date normalization;
- explicit object typing;
- section identity generation according to approved rules.

Normalization shall not alter meaning.

### 11.4 Validate Source

Source validation occurs before enrichment.

It shall detect, at minimum:

- missing required metadata;
- duplicate IDs;
- malformed front matter;
- invalid types;
- invalid predicates;
- broken internal references;
- alias conflicts;
- unsupported schema versions;
- invalid status values.

Build policy determines which findings fail the build and which produce warnings.

### 11.5 Resolve Identities and References

The compiler resolves authored references against stable IDs, aliases, and permitted naming rules.

Resolution shall be deterministic where possible.

Ambiguous references shall not be silently assigned.

They shall be:

- rejected;
- emitted as unresolved;
- or passed to a governed enrichment stage for a proposal.

### 11.6 Extract Deterministic Structure

The compiler derives facts that follow directly from source structure or authored metadata.

Examples:

- repository contains document;
- document contains section;
- section is nested beneath another section;
- document declares an alias;
- authored wiki link references a concept;
- explicit metadata declares a relationship.

These derived objects should normally have full confidence because they follow from authored content or deterministic rules.

### 11.7 Apply Optional Semantic Enrichment

Probabilistic or model-assisted enrichment may be applied after deterministic compilation.

The enrichment stage shall receive constrained inputs such as:

- source section;
- detected entities;
- ontology;
- allowed predicates;
- candidate concepts;
- compiler instructions.

Outputs shall be treated as proposals unless a policy explicitly permits automatic acceptance.

Every enriched object shall record:

- model;
- model version;
- prompt or policy version;
- confidence;
- origin;
- review status;
- source object;
- generation timestamp.

### 11.8 Create Semantic Units

The compiler divides content along semantic boundaries.

The preferred hierarchy is:

```text
Repository
  └── Document
       └── Section
            └── Semantic Knowledge Unit
```

Token limits may require further splitting, but token count alone shall not be the primary chunking strategy.

A semantic unit shall not combine unrelated concepts merely to reach a target size.

### 11.9 Contextualize Embedding Inputs

The compiler may enrich the text used for embedding without modifying the canonical source.

Context may include:

- canonical object name;
- object type;
- document title;
- heading path;
- related concepts;
- relevant aliases;
- domain;
- authority;
- short generated context.

The contextualized embedding input shall be stored or reproducible from metadata.

The original source text shall remain separately identifiable.

### 11.10 Generate Projections

The compiler produces the pack’s logical projections from the normalized model.

Projection generation should be independent enough that one projection can be rebuilt without unnecessarily rebuilding all others.

Examples:

- textual change may rebuild semantic and vector projections;
- authored relationship change may rebuild graph projections;
- ontology change may rebuild affected objects and graph edges;
- embedding-model change may rebuild vector projections only.

### 11.11 Validate Compiled Outputs

Compiled output validation shall verify:

- all referenced canonical IDs exist;
- all required projections are present;
- graph edges use valid predicates;
- vector records reference valid semantic units;
- model and configuration metadata are present;
- manifest inventory matches physical assets;
- content hashes are correct;
- prohibited data is absent according to policy;
- no unresolved fatal diagnostics remain.

### 11.12 Assemble and Publish

Assembly creates a complete candidate pack.

Publication marks the candidate immutable and assigns its final version.

The compiler shall never publish a partially assembled or failed pack as valid.

## 12. Determinism Model

Compilation stages are classified as:

### Deterministic

Given the same source, compiler version, and configuration, the output is expected to be identical.

Examples:

- parsing;
- schema validation;
- stable ID resolution;
- authored relation extraction;
- containment graph generation;
- lexical normalization;
- manifest inventory;
- hashing.

### Controlled nondeterministic

The result may vary because a probabilistic model is used, but the process is governed and traceable.

Examples:

- relationship proposal;
- duplicate detection;
- classification;
- summarization;
- contextualization;
- contradiction detection.

Controlled nondeterministic stages shall not overwrite deterministic outputs.

They shall append governed enrichment with explicit origin and confidence.

## 13. Provenance Model

Every compiled object shall carry or reference a provenance record.

A minimal provenance record contains:

```yaml
source_repository: csu-iq-knowledge
source_revision: <revision>
source_document: frameworks/mcem.md
source_object_id: framework.mcem
source_location: headings/stages/inspire-design
origin: authored | derived | inferred | generated
compiler_version: <version>
transformation: <stage-or-rule-id>
```

Model-assisted objects additionally contain:

```yaml
model_provider: <provider>
model_name: <model>
model_version: <version>
policy_version: <version>
confidence: <0..1>
review_status: proposed | approved | rejected
```

Provenance shall survive every projection.

## 14. Identity Model

Stable IDs form the backbone of the Knowledge Pack.

IDs shall be:

- unique within the defined namespace;
- independent of physical database keys;
- stable across rebuilds;
- human-inspectable where practical;
- explicit for canonical objects;
- deterministically generated only where an approved rule exists.

Representative IDs:

```text
framework.mcem
framework.mcem.stage.inspire-design
role.csam
artifact.success-plan
metric.milestone-health
```

Renaming a title shall not automatically change a stable ID.

Removing or replacing an ID requires explicit lifecycle handling.

## 15. Confidence and Review

Confidence and review are distinct.

Confidence indicates the compiler or model’s estimated certainty.

Review status indicates governance state.

Representative review states:

- proposed;
- approved;
- rejected;
- superseded.

Authored and deterministically derived relationships may be approved by default according to policy.

Probabilistically inferred relationships should default to proposed unless explicitly auto-approved under a narrow, tested rule.

## 16. Validation Policy

Validation findings shall have severity:

- **Error** — build cannot be published.
- **Warning** — build may be published, but the finding is visible.
- **Information** — diagnostic only.

Minimum publication blockers should include:

- duplicate canonical IDs;
- invalid manifest;
- incompatible schema;
- missing mandatory assets;
- invalid ontology references;
- failed content hashes;
- references to nonexistent required objects;
- prohibited operational or customer data;
- incomplete package assembly.

## 17. Versioning Principles

A Knowledge Pack shall expose at least three version dimensions:

### Pack Version

Identifies the published artifact.

### Schema Version

Identifies the structural contract of the pack.

### Source Revision

Identifies the canonical source state used to build the pack.

The ontology and compiler shall also be versioned independently.

A new build shall produce a new pack version whenever any published asset changes.

## 18. Architecture Decision Records — Accepted

### ADR-KP-001 — Canonical knowledge remains outside the Knowledge Pack

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** A compiled artifact must not become a competing authoring surface.

**Decision:** The canonical repository is the sole authority. The pack is a derived representation.

**Consequences:**

- corrections occur in the source;
- every pack can be rebuilt;
- runtime modifications to the pack are prohibited;
- provenance must point back to canonical content.

---

### ADR-KP-002 — Knowledge Packs are immutable published artifacts

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** In-place modification creates ambiguity, weakens traceability, and makes rollback unreliable.

**Decision:** Published packs are immutable. Change creates a new version.

**Consequences:**

- consumers can trust a version identifier;
- package contents are cacheable;
- rollbacks are possible;
- hot-fixing a pack directly is prohibited.

---

### ADR-KP-003 — Knowledge Packs are compiled, not manually assembled

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** Manual assembly cannot reliably preserve validation, provenance, or reproducibility.

**Decision:** Only the Knowledge Compiler produces publishable packs.

**Consequences:**

- compiler correctness becomes critical;
- build automation is required;
- pack creation is testable;
- ad hoc manual edits invalidate the package.

---

### ADR-KP-004 — One canonical model produces multiple derived projections

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** Lexical, graph, semantic, and vector use cases require different optimized representations.

**Decision:** The compiler creates multiple projections from one normalized canonical model.

**Consequences:**

- projections remain synchronized by shared identity;
- duplication is acceptable only as a compiled optimization;
- projections must preserve provenance;
- any projection can be rebuilt.

---

### ADR-KP-005 — Graphs are derived artifacts

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** Treating a graph store as canonical would create a second source of truth.

**Decision:** Graph nodes and edges are projections generated from authored structure, deterministic resolution, and governed enrichment.

**Consequences:**

- the graph can be deleted and recreated;
- graph technology remains replaceable;
- inferred edges require origin and review status;
- direct graph editing is not authoritative.

---

### ADR-KP-006 — Vectors are derived artifacts

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** Embeddings depend on models, configuration, chunking, and contextualization choices.

**Decision:** Vector projections are disposable outputs tied to source IDs and model metadata.

**Consequences:**

- vector indexes can be regenerated;
- embedding models may be replaced;
- model upgrades do not alter canonical knowledge;
- every vector must reference its source unit.

---

### ADR-KP-007 — Deterministic enrichment precedes probabilistic enrichment

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** LLMs should not solve problems that can be resolved through source structure, schemas, aliases, or explicit links.

**Decision:** Deterministic parsing, validation, resolution, and extraction occur before model-assisted enrichment.

**Consequences:**

- builds are more predictable;
- model cost and variability are reduced;
- LLM proposals are constrained by known context;
- deterministic output cannot be overwritten by inferred output.

---

### ADR-KP-008 — LLM-derived knowledge is governed enrichment

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** Model output may be useful but cannot be assumed authoritative.

**Decision:** LLM output is stored with origin, model metadata, confidence, and review status.

**Consequences:**

- inferred knowledge is distinguishable from authored knowledge;
- review workflows can be added later;
- model changes remain auditable;
- silent promotion is prohibited.

---

### ADR-KP-009 — Provenance is preserved at object level

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** A pack must explain where each object and relation came from.

**Decision:** Every compiled object references source and transformation provenance.

**Consequences:**

- evidence can be traced;
- compiler defects can be diagnosed;
- generated content remains distinguishable;
- projection-level storage must preserve object provenance.

---

### ADR-KP-010 — Stable canonical IDs anchor all projections

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** Paths, headings, generated row IDs, and vector positions are not stable identities.

**Decision:** All projections reference stable canonical IDs.

**Consequences:**

- projections can be joined reliably;
- titles and file locations may evolve independently;
- breaking ID changes require explicit handling;
- duplicate IDs fail validation.

---

### ADR-KP-011 — Semantic structure is the primary chunking boundary

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** Fixed token windows often mix unrelated ideas or split coherent knowledge.

**Decision:** Semantic units are formed from document and section meaning. Token limits are a secondary constraint.

**Consequences:**

- chunks are more interpretable;
- retrieval evidence maps cleanly to source sections;
- the parser must preserve structure;
- oversized semantic units may still require controlled subdivision.

---

### ADR-KP-012 — Embedding context is derived without modifying canonical text

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** Embedding quality may improve when text includes document, section, type, or concept context.

**Decision:** The compiler may generate contextual embedding inputs while preserving original source text and provenance.

**Consequences:**

- canonical knowledge remains untouched;
- embedding inputs must be stored or reproducible;
- multiple embeddings may reference one canonical object;
- contextualization strategy must be versioned.

---

### ADR-KP-013 — Knowledge Packs contain no protected customer operational data

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** Knowledge Packs are portable domain artifacts and should not bypass source-system authorization.

**Decision:** Customer-level results, user tokens, RLS outputs, and protected operational records are excluded.

**Consequences:**

- packs are safer to distribute;
- customer context remains outside the pack;
- examples require approval, anonymization, or synthetic data;
- compilation requires prohibited-data checks.

---

### ADR-KP-014 — Physical storage technology does not define the logical pack

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** The architecture must survive technology changes.

**Decision:** Logical assets and contracts are defined independently of DuckDB, SQLite, FAISS, Qdrant, Neo4j, or other engines.

**Consequences:**

- implementations are replaceable;
- conformance tests must target semantics;
- pack schema and physical packaging must be separated;
- technology choices become subordinate ADRs.

## 19. Open Architecture Decisions

### OADR-KP-001 — Physical package layout

**Question:** Should a pack use a single embedded container, a directory of specialized assets, or a hybrid?

**Candidates:**

- single embedded database;
- directory bundle;
- archive containing multiple assets;
- hybrid metadata database plus external indexes.

**Evaluation criteria:**

- atomicity;
- portability;
- inspection;
- update complexity;
- platform support;
- file-locking behavior;
- ease of validation.

---

### OADR-KP-002 — Structured metadata engine

**Question:** Which embedded engine should store normalized objects, metadata, and provenance?

**Candidates:**

- DuckDB;
- SQLite;
- another embedded relational engine.

**Evaluation criteria:**

- Windows support;
- read performance;
- schema evolution;
- full-text capabilities;
- concurrency requirements;
- library footprint;
- operational simplicity.

---

### OADR-KP-003 — Graph physical representation

**Question:** How should the graph projection be materialized?

**Candidates:**

- relational node and edge tables;
- embedded graph library serialization;
- property-graph file;
- RDF representation;
- multiple optional graph outputs.

**Evaluation criteria:**

- traversal performance;
- portability;
- inspectability;
- ontology fit;
- dependency footprint;
- incremental rebuild support.

---

### OADR-KP-004 — Embedded vector representation

**Question:** Which physical vector index format should be used for the initial pack?

**Candidates include:**

- FAISS;
- USearch;
- hnswlib;
- sqlite-vec;
- another embedded index.

**Evaluation criteria:**

- in-process operation;
- CPU performance;
- Windows packaging;
- metadata linkage;
- index portability;
- incremental update support;
- licensing.

Dedicated server products are outside the initial embedded-pack assumption unless scale requirements change.

---

### OADR-KP-005 — Lexical index implementation

**Question:** Which implementation should provide lexical indexing?

**Candidates include:**

- SQLite FTS5;
- DuckDB full-text search;
- Tantivy bindings;
- a Python BM25 implementation;
- a generated inverted index.

**Evaluation criteria:**

- acronym and alias performance;
- ranking quality;
- footprint;
- language support;
- index portability;
- consistency with selected metadata engine.

---

### OADR-KP-006 — Default embedding model

**Question:** Which embedding model should be used as the reference default?

**Candidate families include:**

- BGE;
- E5;
- Nomic Embed;
- Jina Embeddings.

**Evaluation criteria:**

- domain retrieval quality;
- multilingual quality;
- model size;
- CPU inference;
- ONNX availability;
- commercial licensing;
- reproducibility;
- embedding dimensions.

A CSU-IQ-specific benchmark corpus is required before acceptance.

---

### OADR-KP-007 — Multiple embedding representations

**Question:** Should one semantic unit support multiple embedding variants?

**Possible variants:**

- source text;
- contextualized text;
- definition-focused text;
- operational-use text;
- multilingual representation.

**Trade-off:** Better retrieval coverage versus larger packages, more complex scoring, and more expensive compilation.

---

### OADR-KP-008 — Incremental compilation

**Question:** Which stages support incremental rebuild based on content hashes and dependency tracking?

**Potential policy:**

- text change rebuilds semantic and vector projections;
- relationship change rebuilds graph projections;
- ontology change rebuilds affected objects;
- embedding-model change rebuilds vectors only.

**Open issue:** define dependency graph and correctness guarantees.

---

### OADR-KP-009 — Partial and modular packs

**Question:** Can one Knowledge Pack depend on, extend, or compose with another?

**Examples:**

- CSU core domain;
- MCEM extension;
- role-specific extension;
- country-specific extension;
- tool-specific extension.

**Evaluation criteria:**

- ID namespace;
- dependency resolution;
- conflict handling;
- build complexity;
- ontology compatibility.

---

### OADR-KP-010 — Diagnostics placement

**Question:** Are compiler diagnostics embedded in the published pack or emitted only as a separate build report?

**Trade-off:** Runtime inspectability versus minimal package size and separation of concerns.

---

### OADR-KP-011 — Signing and integrity

**Question:** Should v1 require cryptographic signing or only content hashes?

**Candidates:**

- hash-only manifest;
- Ed25519 signatures;
- X.509 signatures;
- enterprise signing backed by Azure Key Vault.

This decision concerns pack integrity only. Distribution and trust-policy enforcement remain outside this SDD.

---

### OADR-KP-012 — Reproducibility threshold for probabilistic enrichment

**Question:** What level of reproducibility is required when enrichment uses nondeterministic models?

**Options:**

- byte-for-byte;
- structurally equivalent;
- semantically equivalent within tests;

## 21. Reference Compiler Components

The reference architecture contains the following logical components:

### Source Loader

Discovers and reads configured canonical sources.

### Parser

Converts source syntax into a loss-aware structured representation.

### Normalizer

Produces the common intermediate representation.

### Schema Validator

Checks source metadata, required fields, types, and constraints.

### Identity Resolver

Resolves stable IDs, aliases, links, and references.

### Ontology Validator

Enforces allowed object types and relationship predicates.

### Deterministic Extractor

Creates containment, authored, and rule-derived knowledge.

### Enrichment Orchestrator

Coordinates optional NLP or LLM enrichment under explicit policies.

### Semantic Unit Builder

Creates coherent knowledge units for semantic and vector projections.

### Projection Builders

Generate structured, graph, lexical, semantic, and vector assets.

### Provenance Recorder

Attaches source and transformation history to compiled objects.

### Pack Assembler

Builds the candidate package and manifest.

### Pack Validator

Runs final conformance and integrity checks.

### Publisher

Marks a validated package immutable and assigns the final version.

## 22. Minimum Definition of Done for v1

A v1 Knowledge Pack is complete when:

1. its canonical source set and source revision are known;
2. all canonical objects have stable IDs;
3. source schemas and ontology are validated;
4. deterministic structure and authored relations are compiled;
5. semantic units are generated from source structure;
6. graph, lexical, and semantic projections are generated;
7. vector projection is generated if enabled by the build profile;
8. every compiled object has provenance;
9. the manifest inventories every asset;
10. content hashes validate;
11. no fatal diagnostics remain;
12. prohibited customer or operational data checks pass;
13. the package is assigned a unique version;
14. the published package is immutable;
15. the package can be rebuilt from recorded inputs.

## 23. Summary

A Knowledge Pack is a compiled, immutable and versioned representation of organizational knowledge.

Its authority comes from its canonical source and its trustworthiness comes from deterministic compilation, governed enrichment, stable identity, validation, and provenance.

The central design rule is:

> Canonical knowledge is authored. Intelligence assets are compiled.

Graphs, vectors, lexical indexes, semantic units, and other optimized representations are products of the compiler. They are never independent truth stores and can always be rebuilt.

The SDD remains intentionally limited to the Knowledge Pack and the principles of producing it.
